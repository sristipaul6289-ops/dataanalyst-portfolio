# Supply Chain Data Analysis Using Python

## Project Overview

This project analyzes 180,519 supply chain and e-commerce order-item records to understand sales, profit, customers, products, and delivery performance.

The analysis was done in Python using Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, and Plotly. The main focus was on finding patterns in business performance and identifying areas that may need further attention.

## Dataset

* 180,519 order items
* 65,752 unique orders
* 20,652 customers
* 118 products
* 5 markets
* 3 customer segments
* 4 shipping modes
* Multiple product categories

## Analysis

The project covers:

* Data understanding and quality checks
* Missing values and duplicate checks
* Date validation
* Numerical and categorical analysis
* Feature engineering
* Sales and profit analysis
* Product and category performance
* Customer analysis
* Delivery and shipping performance
* Market and customer-segment analysis
* Time-based analysis
* Data visualization

New features were created for delivery delay, profit margin, order year, month, quarter, customer order frequency, and order value.

## Key Findings

Sales remained fairly consistent from 2015 to 2017, with 2015 recording the highest sales at approximately 12.34M. A sharp decline was observed in 2018 and requires further investigation.

Fishing was the highest-performing category, generating approximately 6.93M in sales and 756K in profit.

Europe recorded the highest market sales at approximately 10.87M, while Africa recorded the lowest at approximately 2.29M.

Consumer customers generated the highest sales and profit, contributing approximately 19.10M in sales and 2.07M in profit.

Delivery performance was a major concern, with 98,977 order items classified as late deliveries.

Standard Class was the most commonly used shipping method with 107,752 order items, while Second Class showed the highest average delivery delay among the major shipping modes.

The analysis identified 20,652 customers, with 11,768 repeat customers, giving a repeat customer rate of 56.98%.

Customer analysis also showed that high sales do not always mean high profitability, making customer profit an important metric alongside revenue.
